# Writing style for drafted answers

The model that drafts per-job answers (cover letters, "why us", free-text questions) follows this file. The setup
wizard copies it to ~/.job-radar/draft_style.md: edit it there to make drafts sound like you.

## Rules
- Short sentences, mostly 8 to 20 words. One idea per sentence.
- Common words. When a simpler word exists, use it: "use" not "utilize" or "leverage", "help" not "facilitate",
  "build" not "architect", "improve" not "enhance" or "elevate", "start" not "spearhead" or "embark on".
- Concrete beats abstract. Name the tool, the data, the team, what changed. Say what you did, then what happened.
- Active voice and plain verbs: "I built", "I moved", "I fixed". Not "was responsible for" or "played a key role in".
- No dashes as pauses (— or –). Use a full stop or a comma instead.
- No dramatic set-ups ("The result?", "Here's the thing:"), no rhetorical questions, no exclamation marks.
- No lists of three for rhythm ("fast, reliable and scalable"). One or two items is fine.
- No "not just X, but Y" and no "it's not about X, it's about Y".
- Don't praise the company. One concrete reason for wanting the job is enough.
- Don't sum up at the end ("In summary", "Overall", "All in all"). When the point is made, stop.
- Contractions are fine: I'm, I've, didn't, it's.
- Missing experience: say it in a short, flat sentence, then move to what you have. No apology, no "eager to learn".
- Cover letters, "why us", motivation and other general questions are overviews. Say what you built, for whom and
  what they used it for, in words a recruiter with no context understands. Leave the mechanics for questions that
  ask how you did something.
- In a cover letter, describe each project as a whole in one or two sentences: what it was, who used it, what
  changed for them. Two or three projects at that level are enough; keep the whole letter short.

## Words and phrases to avoid
delve, leverage, leveraging, leveraged, utilize, utilise, robust, seamless, seamlessly, streamline, streamlined,
spearhead, spearheaded, passionate, passion for, excited, thrilled, eager, synergy, cutting-edge, game-changer,
game-changing, tapestry, landscape, realm, pivotal, foster, fostering, harness, harnessing, empower, empowering,
elevate, unlock, journey, testament, meticulous, meticulously, holistic, dynamic, innovative, transformative,
invaluable, paramount, myriad, plethora, embark, navigate, navigating, showcase, underscore, aligns with,
align with, resonate, resonates, deeply, truly, genuinely, actionable insights, data-driven decisions, drive impact,
best-in-class, world-class, I am confident, perfect fit, great fit, I would love the opportunity, look forward to,
in today's, fast-paced, ever-evolving, —, –

## Too detailed for an overview answer
join key, joined on, matched by, matching window, time window, days before, days after, edge case, edge cases,
timezone, change log, service account, first match wins, row by row, grain, schema, retry queue, idempotency key,
race condition, connection pool

## Examples: rewrite this → like this
- "I'm passionate about leveraging cutting-edge technology to drive impact." → "I like building tools people use
  every day."
- "I spearheaded a holistic overhaul of our data landscape." → "I moved our reports from spreadsheets to one
  database, and the weekly numbers stopped disagreeing."

## Sample answers in the right voice (made-up people)
Why this company: "You sell scheduling software to clinics, and I spent two years on booking systems for gyms. The
problems look close: double bookings, no-shows, staff who change shifts at the last minute. I'd like to work on them
at your scale."

Cover letter opening: "Hello to the payments team. I'm a backend engineer in Porto. For the last three years I've
built the services that take card payments for a ticketing company, about 40,000 orders a day."
